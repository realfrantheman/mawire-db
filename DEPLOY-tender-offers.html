What Is a Tender Offer?

A tender offer is a public bid by an acquirer to purchase shares directly from a target company's shareholders at a specified price per share, typically at a premium to the current market price. Unlike a merger negotiated with the target's board, a tender offer goes directly to shareholders.

Under Section 14(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, any acquirer making a tender offer for more than 5% of a registered company's shares must file Form SC TO-T with the Securities and Exchange Commission. This filing discloses the offer price, conditions, expiration date, withdrawal rights, and all financing details.

Tender offers are used in both friendly and hostile acquisitions. In a friendly deal, the target's board has already agreed to the acquisition. In a hostile bid, the acquirer goes directly to shareholders without board approval, often triggering a public battle for control of the company.

How to Read an SC TO-T Filing
Offer Price Per Share

The price the acquirer is offering for each share. Usually includes a premium over the 30-day or 60-day volume-weighted average price to incentivise shareholders to tender.

Minimum Tender Condition

The minimum percentage of shares that must be tendered for the offer to proceed — typically 50% plus one share, or a higher threshold required for a short-form merger.

Regulatory Conditions

Antitrust clearances (HSR Act), CFIUS review for foreign acquirers, and any industry-specific regulatory approvals required before the tender offer can close.

Expiration Date

Tender offers must remain open for at least 20 US business days. Shareholders can withdraw tendered shares at any time before the expiration date.
