About Financial Services M&A Deals

Financial services is one of the most active M&A sectors globally. Bank consolidation, insurance company mergers, asset manager roll-ups, payment company acquisitions, and the ongoing digital transformation of financial services through fintech M&A have produced sustained deal activity across three decades.

Every financial services merger on mergers.news is sourced from an official SEC regulatory filing. Bank and financial institution mergers are subject to extensive regulatory disclosure requirements under the Securities Exchange Act, making the EDGAR database particularly comprehensive for this sector.

The 2008 financial crisis produced some of the largest distressed bank acquisitions in history. The current environment of rising rates, fintech disruption, and digital banking transformation is driving a new wave of consolidation.

Notable Financial Services M&A Deals
Bank of America / Merrill Lynch — $50B

2009 · BofA acquired Merrill Lynch during the financial crisis, creating the largest US retail brokerage combined with a major investment bank.

S&P Global / IHS Markit — $44B

2022 · The merger of two financial data giants created a dominant provider of financial information, indices, and analytics services globally.

Charles Schwab / TD Ameritrade — $26B

2020 · The merger of the two largest US discount brokers created an industry-dominant retail investment platform with $6T in client assets.

JPMorgan Chase / Bear Stearns — $1.2B

2008 · Emergency acquisition of failing Bear Stearns during the financial crisis, facilitated by the Federal Reserve with $29B in asset guarantees.

Blackstone / Hilton Hotels — $26B

2007 · The largest private equity LBO of the hotel sector, eventually generating $14B in profit for Blackstone on exit.

Visa / Plaid — $5.3B (blocked)

2021 · Visa's proposed acquisition of fintech data network Plaid was abandoned after the DOJ filed antitrust suit — one of the most watched fintech deals ever.

Sub-Sectors
Commercial Banking

Bank consolidation has been a multi-decade trend. The number of US commercial banks has fallen from 14,000 in 1985 to under 5,000 today through mergers and failures.

Fintech

Financial technology acquisitions have surged as traditional banks acquire digital payment, lending, and wealth management capabilities.

Insurance

Insurance consolidation driven by scale economics and private equity buyouts of specialty and life insurance companies.

Asset Management

Asset manager roll-ups driven by fee compression and the shift to passive investing. BlackRock and private equity firms are the most active consolidators.
