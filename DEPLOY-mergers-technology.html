About Technology M&A

The technology sector consistently leads global M&A activity by both volume and value. Software consolidation, semiconductor scale-up, and AI capability acquisition have driven hundreds of billions in annual deal flow since the late 1990s. The dot-com era, the mobile revolution, the cloud transition, and now the AI arms race have each produced distinct waves of tech M&A activity.

Every technology merger and acquisition on mergers.news is sourced from an official SEC regulatory filing — primarily DEFM14A definitive merger proxy statements filed by target companies, SC TO-T tender offers filed by acquirers, and S-4 merger registration statements. These filings contain the full merger agreement, per-share consideration, fairness opinions, and all material deal terms.

Unlike news-based databases, mergers.news data originates from the primary legal source — the SEC filing itself — ensuring accuracy and completeness of transaction details.

Notable Technology Acquisitions
Microsoft / Activision Blizzard — $68.7B

2023 · The largest gaming acquisition in history. Microsoft acquired Call of Duty publisher Activision after an 18-month regulatory battle across US, EU, and UK authorities.

Broadcom / VMware — $61B

2023 · The largest enterprise software acquisition ever completed. Broadcom acquired cloud infrastructure leader VMware after clearing global antitrust review.

Microsoft / LinkedIn — $26.2B

2016 · Microsoft's largest acquisition at the time. LinkedIn was acquired to integrate professional networking with Microsoft's enterprise software suite.

Dell / EMC — $67B

2016 · The largest technology acquisition ever at the time of closing. Dell acquired storage giant EMC including its VMware stake to create an integrated IT infrastructure company.

Nvidia / Mellanox — $6.9B

2020 · Nvidia acquired networking specialist Mellanox to accelerate its data centre strategy ahead of the AI boom that followed.

Salesforce / Slack — $27.7B

2021 · Salesforce acquired workplace messaging platform Slack to compete with Microsoft Teams and deepen its enterprise software portfolio.

Technology M&A Sub-Sectors
Artificial Intelligence

AI acquisitions have surged since 2022. Major buyers include Microsoft (OpenAI investment), Google (DeepMind, various), Amazon (Anthropic investment), and hundreds of strategic acquirers building AI capabilities.

Cybersecurity

Security consolidation accelerated post-pandemic. Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, and private equity firms have been prolific acquirers of endpoint, cloud, and identity security companies.

Semiconductors

Chip industry consolidation driven by scale requirements. Nvidia/ARM (blocked), AMD/Xilinx, Intel/Mobileye, and Broadcom/Qualcomm attempts defined the decade.

Cloud & SaaS

Software-as-a-Service consolidation produced some of the largest tech deals. Salesforce, SAP, Oracle, and private equity rollups have been the most active acquirers.
