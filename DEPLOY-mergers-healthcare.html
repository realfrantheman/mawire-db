About Healthcare M&A Deals

Healthcare is the second-largest M&A sector globally by value. Pharmaceutical patent cliffs, biotechnology innovation, hospital system consolidation, and medical technology integration have driven sustained deal activity across every economic cycle since the 1990s.

Every healthcare merger on mergers.news is sourced from an official SEC regulatory filing — DEFM14A proxy statements seeking shareholder approval, SC TO-T tender offers, and S-4 merger registrations. These filings contain the complete merger agreement, per-share consideration, fairness opinions from investment banking advisors, and all regulatory approval conditions.

Pharmaceutical and biotech M&A is particularly well-represented in the SEC EDGAR database due to the public company nature of most major acquirers and targets in the US market.

Notable Healthcare M&A Deals
Bristol-Myers Squibb / Celgene — $74B

2019 · BMS acquired cancer drug specialist Celgene in the largest biopharma merger in history, gaining Revlimid and a deep oncology pipeline.

AbbVie / Allergan — $63B

2020 · AbbVie acquired botox maker Allergan in the largest pharmaceutical acquisition of the decade, diversifying beyond its Humira blockbuster drug.

CVS Health / Aetna — $69B

2018 · CVS acquired insurance giant Aetna to create a vertically integrated healthcare company combining pharmacy, insurance, and clinic operations.

Pfizer / Wyeth — $68B

2009 · Pfizer's acquisition of Wyeth during the financial crisis remains one of the largest pharmaceutical mergers ever completed.

Amgen / Horizon Therapeutics — $27.8B

2023 · Amgen acquired rare disease drug maker Horizon after an FTC challenge, adding treatments for thyroid eye disease and gout.

UnitedHealth / Change Healthcare — $13B

2022 · UnitedHealth's acquisition of healthcare technology company Change Healthcare completed after DOJ antitrust review.

Sub-Sectors
Pharmaceuticals

Big pharma acquisitions driven by patent cliffs and pipeline needs. Pfizer, AbbVie, Merck, and J&J are the most prolific acquirers.

Biotechnology

Biotech buyouts accelerated as large pharma companies seek clinical-stage assets. Oncology, rare disease, and gene therapy are the most active areas.

Medical Devices

Device consolidation driven by hospital cost pressure. Medtronic, Abbott, and Stryker have been the most active acquirers.

Health Insurance

Insurer consolidation and vertical integration with providers and pharmacy benefit managers has reshaped US healthcare delivery.
